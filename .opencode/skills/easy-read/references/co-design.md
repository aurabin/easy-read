# Co-design without contacts

You do not need personal contacts to start. You do need to be honest about what
you have and have not tested.

## Status rule

Until the people this is for have used this, describe it as:

> Based on published Easy Read guidance. Not yet tested with the people it is
> for.

Do not call it "accessible" or "approved". Do not claim that the target group
chose or approved it. Say "designed for" and "untested".

## What you can do alone

1. Follow published guidance. Read the Easy Read guidance from Inclusion
   Europe, Mencap, and the UK Government Service Manual.
2. Compare with real examples. Find Easy Read documents that organizations
   publish. Check your output against them by eye and with the checker.
3. Run the checker. It catches long sentences, hard words, passive voice,
   idioms, and long words. It does not catch confusing meaning.
4. Read it out loud. If you run out of breath, the sentence is too long.
5. Ask any reader. A colleague, friend, or family member can say "I do not
   understand this part". That is not user testing, but it finds real problems.
6. Keep the rules editable. The person or group who tests later may need
   different rules.

## Who to ask

- Local self-advocacy groups, such as a People First group.
- Advocacy organizations for people with intellectual disabilities in your
  country.
- Companies or teams that produce Easy Read translations.
- Universities that do inclusive or participatory research.
- Plain-language panels and accessibility meetups.
- Online communities run by and for people with intellectual disabilities.

Support workers and family members can help you make contact. Their opinion is
not a substitute for the person's own opinion.

## What to ask for

Ask for 30 to 45 minutes of feedback. Offer payment. Use a familiar place or a
video call. Let the person bring a supporter. Take breaks.

Say this first:

> I made a tool that writes in Easy Read. I want to know if it is clear. You are
> helping me test the tool, not being tested yourself. You can stop at any time.

## Email template

```text
Subject: Feedback on a free Easy Read writing tool

Hello,

I am building a free writing tool for OpenCode. It is meant to produce
Easy Read text. I have followed published Easy Read guidance, but I have
not tested it with the people it is for yet.

Could someone from your group try it for 30 minutes and tell me what is
clear and what is confusing? I can pay the usual rate for consultation.
I can come to you, or meet by video call. A supporter is welcome.

I will send the questions in advance in plain language.

Thank you,
[your name]
[contact]
```

## How to test

1. Give 3 short, realistic tasks. Example: "Ask the tool to explain an error
   message. Then tell me what you would do next."
2. Ask about understanding, not taste. Good questions:
   - "What does this tell you to do?"
   - "Which part is confusing?"
   - "What would you do first?"
   - "Can you say it in your own words?"
3. Do not ask "Is this easy?" People often say yes to be polite.
4. Do not defend the tool. Write down the exact words the person uses.
5. Watch where they stop, re-read, or ask for help.
6. Ask permission before recording.
7. Test with more than one person. Three to five is a useful start.

## After the test

- List the changes you will make.
- Tell the people who helped what you changed.
- Add their feedback to the project notes, with permission.
- Run the test again with new people after big changes.

## First checklist

- [ ] The README and any description say "not yet tested".
- [ ] The word "accessible" is not used as a claim.
- [ ] There is a way for people to send feedback.
- [ ] The rules are easy to change.
- [ ] There is a budget or a plan to pay testers.
- [ ] A plain-language information sheet and consent note exist.
- [ ] A first task list with 3 tasks is ready.
- [ ] At least one group has been contacted.

## Plain-language information sheet

Copy this and change it. Keep it short. Read it out loud.

```text
About this test

We made a writing tool. It is called Easy Read mode.
We want to know if it is clear.

You will try 3 small tasks. It takes about 30 minutes.
You can take a break. You can stop at any time.
Stopping is okay. Nothing bad will happen.

We will write down what you say. We will not record you
unless you say yes.

We will pay you [amount] for your time.
Your name will not be shared unless you say yes.

You can ask us questions at any time.
Contact: [name, email, phone]
```

Ask the person to say yes in their own way. If they use a supporter or AAC,
let them use it. A signature is not the only way to agree.
