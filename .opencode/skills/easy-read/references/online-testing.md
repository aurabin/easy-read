# Online testing

You can get real feedback without leaving your home. This file lists routes
that work online. Checked in November 2026. Prices and availability change.

## Route 1: Pay for an online review

This is the fastest reliable route. You pay, they find the readers, and you get
feedback in 1 to 3 weeks.

| Service | What you get | Cost | Contact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Easy Read Online (UK) | Document review with an online feedback session. Testers with learning disabilities are paid. | Quote | enquiries@easy-read-online.co.uk |
| A2i (UK) | Easy Read User Review Service. Works with Misfits Theatre, a group of adults with learning disabilities. | £800 + VAT | 01179 44 00 44, a2i.co.uk |
| Information Access Group (AU) | Two in-house testers with intellectual disability, or one-on-one remote interviews. | Quote in 24 h | info@informationaccessgroup.com |
| Fable (remote panel) | Cognitive accessibility testing: self-guided tasks and interviews with testers who have cognitive disabilities. | From $3,750 USD | makeitfable.com |
| Accessibility.build | 3 participants from the target group, one task flow, remote sessions. | From $2,400 USD | accessibility.build |

Ask each one the same questions:

- Do the testers have intellectual disabilities?
- Is the session live, recorded, or written?
- Can I send my own text and screenshots?
- What do I get back, and when?
- Can you sign a simple agreement about privacy?

## Route 2: Ask a network for a free introduction

These organisations may not test for you. They can point you to a member group
that does.

- **DS International** — global network of 150+ organisations.
  Contact form: https://ds-int.org/contact-us. Phone: +44 1395 904200.
  Ask for the member organisation in your country.
- **Inclusion Europe** — proofreads easy-to-read in English and French.
  secretariat@inclusion-europe.org. Its member list includes Easy Read
  producers. Example: Büro für Leichte Sprache, Lebenshilfe Bremen,
  leichte-sprache@lebenshilfe-bremen.de.
- **Disability Equality Scotland** — runs an Easy Read user feedback group and
  the UK Easy Read Standards. easyread@disabilityequality.scot.
- **Deutsches InfoCenter** — see `co-design.md`.

## Route 3: Recruit directly online

Free, but slower and easy to get wrong.

1. Ask the moderator or admin **before** you post.
2. Write a short plain-language post:
   - what the tool does;
   - that it is not tested yet;
   - 30 minutes, online, supporter welcome;
   - what you pay;
   - how to contact you.
3. Do not send direct messages to people you do not know.
4. Do not ask people to describe their support needs in public.
5. Do not use a support group as free labour. Pay, or offer something real.
6. Use an organisation email address, not your personal one.

## Route 4: Run it yourself over video

- 30 minutes. Zoom or Teams. Captions on. Camera optional.
- Let the person bring a supporter.
- Share your screen. Ask them to say what they see.
- Record only with clear consent. Delete it when the work is done.
- Pay the group's usual rate, or offer a voucher.

## Async test kit

Use this when a live call is too much. It takes about 15 minutes.

Send:

1. Three short tasks. Example: "Read this answer. Then tell me what you would
   do next."
2. Five questions, one per screen:
   - What does this tell you to do?
   - Which word is hard to understand?
   - What would you do first?
   - Where did you get stuck?
   - What should we change?
3. A choice of reply: written form, voice note, or a short call.
4. The information sheet and consent text from `co-design.md`.

Do not ask "Is this easy?" Ask "What is confusing?"

## What counts as tested

Be exact.

| What happened | What you may say |
| --- | --- |
| Nobody from the target group used it | "Not yet tested" |
| One person reviewed it | "Reviewed by one person from the target group" |
| 3 to 5 people tried tasks | "Small user test, 3 to 5 people" |
| A group reviewed it over time | "Co-designed with [group]" |

Always say how many people, when, and what you changed.

## If you want to stay out of it

You do not have to appear on camera, give your name, or join a call.

- Use a service. They recruit, run the session, and send a written report. You
  send text and screenshots. You never attend.
- Use a project email address, not your personal one. A pseudonym is fine.
- Ask for written feedback only. Say no to video and voice.
- Let the organisation handle consent and payment. Ask what they will share
  with you, and what they will keep private.
- Do not put your name, photo, or location in the project.
- If you want no contact at all: do not recruit. Keep the "not yet tested"
  label and treat it as a personal tool. That is allowed.

Testing still needs at least one real person from the target group. You cannot
test with nobody. But your body and identity can stay out of the room.

## Safety and privacy

- Collect only what you need. Do not ask for diagnoses.
- Do not publish names, photos, or recordings without written consent.
- Delete recordings and forms when the work is done.
- Do not promise money you cannot pay.
- Public posts attract scammers. Use organisation contacts.

## Short English email

```text
Subject: Online feedback on an Easy Read tool (30 minutes, paid)

Hello,

I built a free writing tool that produces Easy Read text.
It is based on published guidance, but it is not yet tested with the people
it is for.

Could your group try it online for 30 minutes and tell me what is clear
and what is confusing? A supporter is welcome. I will pay your usual rate
and send the questions in advance in plain language.

I can send a short information sheet and a consent note.

Thank you,
[your name]
[contact]
```
