# Lesson 8: Make money in all aspects

**Time:** 40 minutes. **You need:** lessons 4 to 7. A notebook.

## First, the limit

This is not financial, tax, or legal advice.
Rules differ by country. Ask an accountant and a lawyer.
Do not quit a job or borrow money after one lesson.

## The first question

Who pays, and for what?

Money follows value. Name the problem you solve.
A clear offer says who, what, when, and how much.

Weak: "I do AI and accessibility."
Strong: "I make one document easier to read in 5 days."

## The four kinds of income

1. **Time.** You sell hours or days.
2. **Result.** You sell a finished piece of work.
3. **Product.** You sell the same file or tool many times.
4. **Access.** You sell support, updates, or a community.

Most businesses mix two or three.
A product still needs support. A service can become a product.

Pick one route for the next 30 days. Not five.

## Pricing

A price has to cover more than your time.

Add these before you name a price:

1. Your time.
2. Your costs.
3. Tax.
4. Payment fees.
5. Refunds and bad debts.
6. A margin for risk.

That number is your floor. Do not go below it.
A cheap job can cost you money and block better work.

Common models:

- **Hourly:** simple, but it rewards slow work.
- **Fixed price:** clear, but scope can grow.
- **Value-based:** fair for big results, hard to prove.
- **Retainer:** steady, but hours can go unused.
- **Subscription:** recurring, but people leave.
- **Pay what you can:** wide access, low income.
- **Free plus paid:** wide reach, two products to run.

## Costs and paperwork

Keep business money separate from private money.
Keep every receipt. Use one invoice template.

A written agreement should say:

- what you will do;
- what you will not do;
- the price and the tax;
- the dates;
- when payment is due;
- who owns the result;
- how either side can cancel;
- how you handle private data.

An invoice needs a number, a date, a description, and the tax.
Ask a professional to check one template. Then reuse it.

## Ethics

- No fake scarcity.
- No hidden fees.
- No dark patterns.
- No selling personal data.
- No paywall on information people need to stay safe.
- Offer a free or low-cost route for people who cannot pay.
- Say what is free and what is paid.
- Do not call a product "accessible" before real users test it.
- Pay testers and collaborators.
- Do not use a person's story to sell without permission.

## Accessible money

The money side must also be easy to use.

- Offer more than one payment route.
- Do not force a card. Offer an invoice or a bank transfer.
- Write the invoice in plain language.
- Show the total before the last step.
- Make refunds clear.
- Do not force an account for a one-time purchase.
- Answer by email, not only by phone.
- Keep the page usable with a keyboard and a screen reader.

## First 30 days

1. Pick one route.
2. Name one customer or reader.
3. Write one offer in five lines.
4. Set a price above your floor.
5. Ask five people. Do not mass-message.
6. Do one paid pilot.
7. Send one invoice.
8. Ask for one piece of feedback.
9. Decide: repeat, change, or stop.

## Red flags

- "Guaranteed passive income."
- "Get rich with AI in 30 days."
- "No work needed."
- "Buy now or lose the price."
- "Keep this income secret."
- A client who refuses a written agreement.

## Try this

Write one offer in five lines:

1. Who it is for.
2. The problem.
3. The result.
4. The price.
5. The time.

Ask one person to read it.
Ask: "Is it clear who this is for?"
