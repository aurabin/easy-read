# Start something from scratch

You do not need money, a team, or permission to start.
You need one small step, and one person who says: "try it".

## An example that worked

[AROS](http://aros.org/) is a free operating system. It started in 1995 with a
few people who wanted a system like the Amiga. Today many people build it
together. It is still not finished. It is still growing.

That is the point. A project can start with one idea and grow slowly. Nobody
waits until it is perfect.

Your idea does not have to be big. A one-page newsletter for your club is a
real project.

## Ten small ideas

Pick one. Start with one sentence.

| Idea | What to type |
| --- | --- |
| A newsletter for your group | `/easy write a one-page newsletter for our group` |
| A guide to a place you know | `/steps explain how to visit our office` |
| A letter about one problem | `/easy write a letter about the broken lift` |
| A club for something you like | `/easy write an invitation for a chess club` |
| A recipe collection | `/easy write down my grandmother's recipe` |
| A survey for your town | `/easy make five questions about our town` |
| A repair café or swap meet | `/easy write a plan for a repair café` |
| A story about your life | `/easy start my story` |
| Voice notes about your week | `/steps plan a short voice note` |
| A small website | `/easy explain how to make a simple website` |

## Why Easy Read helps here

If more people can read your text, more people can join your project.
Easy Read is not only for official papers. It is for invitations, plans, and
rules too.

## What I will not do

I will not use your story to make this tool look good.
You decide what to share and what stays private.
If you want to start something and not be named, that is fine.

## A note on the example

AROS is a computer project, not a support project. It is here because it
shows the shape of a project that started small. It is not proof that this
tool works. Nothing is, yet.
