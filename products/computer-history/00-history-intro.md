# Computer History: A Short Course

**Learn the story. Then run the real machines.**

This course tells a short history of computers.
Then it lets you visit two famous machines yourself.
You use free emulators. You do not need old hardware.

**Time:** about 3 hours in total. **You need:** a computer and a browser.
**Level:** 0+. No prior knowledge. Each lesson is short.

## How this course works

1. Read the story below. It is short.
2. Visit the Commodore 64. Six short lessons.
3. Visit the Amiga. Five short lessons.
4. Stop after any lesson. One lesson counts.

You do not have to finish. You can come back any time.

## The story, in seven steps

### 1. Computers filled rooms

The first computers were huge. They filled whole rooms.
This was in the 1940s. They used parts called vacuum tubes.
They were hot, slow, and very expensive.
Only governments and big labs had them.

### 2. A small part changed everything

In 1947, a new part was invented. It is called the transistor.
It did the same job as a vacuum tube. But it was small and cool.
Computers got smaller. More places could buy one.

### 3. Many parts on one chip

In the 1960s, makers put many transistors on one chip.
A chip is a small flat piece of silicon.
This made computers smaller again. And cheaper.

### 4. The whole brain on one chip

In 1971, the first microprocessor arrived.
It put the main parts of a computer on one chip.
Now a computer could be small enough for a desk.
This opened the door to home computers.

### 5. Computers came home

In the late 1970s, home computers appeared.
The Apple II, the Commodore PET, and the TRS-80 came in 1977.
People could buy a computer for the home.
You could type on it, play, and learn to program.

### 6. The Commodore 64

In 1982, Commodore released the Commodore 64.
It was cheap. It had good sound and graphics for the price.
It sold in millions. It is one of the best-selling computers ever.
Many people learned to program on it. You will visit it next.

### 7. The Amiga

In 1985, Commodore released the Amiga.
It had advanced graphics and sound.
It had a desktop and a mouse. It could do several jobs at once.
It was ahead of its time. You will visit it after the C64.

## What came after

Later, IBM-style PCs and Windows became common.
Commodore stopped in 1994. But the machines live on.
Fans still use them, make music, and write new programs.
Today your phone is a computer too. It is far more powerful.
But the story started with those room-sized machines.

## Why visit the old machines

You can read history. Or you can touch it.
An emulator is a program that acts like the old machine.
It runs on your computer. It is free for the C64.
You type the same commands people typed in the 1980s.
That makes the history real. You learn by doing.
